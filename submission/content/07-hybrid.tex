\section{Where the quantum method sits, and why there}
\label{sec:hybrid}

\textbf{Classical, on all traffic.} A gradient-boosted scorer runs on every transaction --- the
only component that sees full volume, and deliberately the strongest available baseline rather
than a straw man, at \ClaimGbdtFullAucMin--\ClaimGbdtFullAucMax{} ROC AUC over five seeds. A
hybrid design whose classical half is weak proves nothing about its quantum half.

\textbf{Conformal, on the decision.} The certificate wraps whatever scores the band, so the
deliverable an issuer can act on survives a negative quantum result --- as it had to here.

\textbf{Quantum, on the band only.} The abstention band is a few percent of traffic, which is
why a quantum re-ranker is conceivable here and nowhere else in the pipeline. At a
\ClaimTestFraudRate\,\% positive rate and a 3-D Secure challenge budget the band holds
thousands of rows rather than hundreds of thousands, and a kernel whose Gram matrix costs
$O(n^2)$ circuit evaluations is affordable on thousands. The band is not a convenience; it is
the only place the arithmetic works.

\textbf{The compute path.} Both quantum arms run on simulators, which the statement permits and
does not penalise: no number in this proposal comes from a QPU. The fidelity
kernel's reference implementation is an exact statevector, with Braket's
\texttt{LocalSimulator} and Aer on CPU and GPU agreeing to within
\ClaimParityWorstDifference{} over \ClaimParityComparisons{} comparisons. The tensor network
runs in PyTorch on a single GPU, bound by kernel-launch overhead rather than arithmetic
(Section~\ref{sec:quantum}). On the sample count the statement asks for: the screens ran on
\ClaimScreenSamples{} stratified rows per configuration across \ClaimScreenConfigs{}
configurations, and the kernel arm was rejected before any downstream evaluation, so no larger
quantum execution took place. The circuits are within near-term reach --- at most
\ClaimCircuitQubitsMax{} qubits, transpiled depth \ClaimCircuitDepthMax{} and
\ClaimCircuitTwoQubitMax{} two-qubit gates on a portable basis --- so what stopped this arm was
the screens, not the hardware.
