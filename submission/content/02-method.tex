\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Paradigm.} Hybrid classical-quantum: a \emph{gate-based} fidelity kernel and a
\emph{quantum-inspired} matrix-product-state classifier, both confined to an abstention band and
wrapped in a classical certificate that depends on neither.

\paragraph{The rule, and which block may touch which parameter.} Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}
is the whole method. A classical scorer $f$ splits traffic three ways against thresholds fixed
in advance; the band is $B = \{x : \taulo \le f(x) < \tauhi\}$ and $g$ is the in-band
re-scorer, where a quantum model would enter if one were usable. None was, so the certified rule
takes $g = f$ and $\lambda$ is a third threshold on the same score. Which block sets what
carries the guarantee: edges and thresholds from $\Dband$, $\lambda$ certified on $\Dcal$,
nothing selected on $\Dtest$.

\paragraph{The estimand is band-conditional, deliberately.} We certify
\[
R(\lambda) \;=\; \mathbb{P}\bigl(D(X) = \textsc{decline} \,\bigm|\, Y = 0,\; X \in B\bigr),
\]
not the marginal false-decline rate. The marginal rate is dominated by $\tauhi$ and would be
almost unchanged if $g$ were replaced by a coin flip. A certificate that does not bind the
component it is said to license is not evidence about that component --- so the harder,
smaller-sample quantity is the one we bound, and the cost of that choice is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:results} rather than hidden.

\paragraph{Two distinct breaks of exchangeability, and the split separates them.} The blocks are
60/10/10/20\,\%, contiguous and snapped to day boundaries. The \textbf{temporal} break is that
$\Dcal$ precedes $\Dtest$. The \textbf{selective} break is that band membership is a
data-dependent event, if the edges were estimated on the data used to certify. Freezing the
edges on $\Dband$ removes the second by construction: membership becomes a fixed predicate, and
filtering by one preserves exchangeability of what remains. Only the temporal break survives,
and we measure it.

\paragraph{Risk control.} Learn-then-Test controls family-wise error over a finite grid fixed
in advance, using Hoeffding--Bentkus $p$-values with Holm. Two risks are controlled jointly:
$R(\lambda)$ at level $\alpha$, and the false-negative rate at $\alpha_{\mathrm{FN}}$. A grid
point is admissible only when both nulls are rejected, so the joint statement inherits one
confidence: $\mathbb{P}\bigl(R(\lambda) \le \alpha\bigr) \ge 1 - \delta$ at $\delta = 0.05$.

The bound applies only to a \emph{mean of per-observation losses}; our first formulation was a
nonlinear transform of one and produced invalid $p$-values. It is now a $0/1$ loss averaged over
frauds, a recall floor at $1 - \alpha_{\mathrm{FN}}$.

\paragraph{Coverage is judged against the exact law, not against $\alpha$.} For a sound
split-conformal system the test-error count follows
$E \sim \mathrm{BetaBinomial}(m,\ n{+}1{-}k,\ k)$ with $k = \lceil (n{+}1)(1-\alpha) \rceil$.
Checking $\hat r \le \alpha$ is a one-sided test against the wrong null: on a correctly
calibrated system it passes only about half the time. Every verdict below is the two-sided
Beta-Binomial interval at the 99\,\% level.

\paragraph{Imbalance is absorbed by thresholds, not by resampling.} At a
\ClaimTestFraudRate\,\% positive rate that is a choice, and the guarantee makes it: resampling
the calibration block would break the exchangeability the certificate needs, so every block is
scored as it falls and the imbalance is carried by where $\taulo$, $\lambda$ and $\tauhi$ sit.

\paragraph{Intervals resample entities, not rows.} The IEEE-CIS label propagates a reported
chargeback to later transactions sharing an account, email or billing address, so rows are
clustered: \ClaimCardOverlap\,\% of test-block \texttt{card1} values also appear in training.
Every interval is a card-level block bootstrap.
