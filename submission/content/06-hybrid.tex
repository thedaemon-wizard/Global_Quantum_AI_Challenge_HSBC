\section{Where the quantum method sits, and why there}

The architecture is hybrid by necessity rather than by fashion, and the division of labour is
what makes the quantum component affordable at all.

\textbf{Classical, on all traffic.} A gradient-boosted scorer runs on every transaction. It is
the only component that sees full volume, and it is deliberately the strongest available
baseline rather than a straw man --- tuned, five seeds, and reported at
\ClaimGbdtFullAucMin--\ClaimGbdtFullAucMax{} ROC AUC. A hybrid design whose classical half is
weak proves nothing about its quantum half.

\textbf{Conformal, on the decision.} The certificate is classical statistics and stays that
way. It wraps whatever scores the band, so the guarantee does not depend on the quantum
component existing, working, or being run on hardware. That is a deliberate ordering: the
deliverable an issuer can act on survives a negative quantum result, which is what happened.

\textbf{Quantum, on the band only.} The abstention band is a few percent of traffic, which is
the reason a quantum re-ranker is conceivable here and nowhere else in the pipeline. At a
\ClaimTestFraudRate\,\% positive rate and a 3-D Secure challenge budget, the band holds
thousands of rows rather than hundreds of thousands, and a kernel whose Gram matrix costs
$O(n^2)$ circuit evaluations is affordable on thousands. The band is not a convenience; it is
the only place the arithmetic works.

\textbf{The compute path.} Both quantum arms run on simulators, which the challenge statement
permits explicitly and does not penalise. The fidelity kernel is evaluated on an exact
statevector backend with Braket's \texttt{LocalSimulator} as the reference implementation and
Aer CPU and GPU as cross-checks, which agree to floating-point round-off. The tensor network runs
in PyTorch on a single GPU, where it is bound by kernel-launch overhead rather than arithmetic
--- per-step cost is flat across the bond dimensions measured, despite a thousandfold
change in FLOPs. That measurement is what makes a seed-replicated sweep affordable, and it is reported
because it is the sort of thing a Phase II resource estimate needs and rarely has.

\textbf{Quantum execution scale.} The challenge statement requires the sample count used for
quantum execution to be stated. The screens ran on \ClaimScreenSamples{} stratified rows per
configuration across \ClaimScreenConfigs{} configurations; the kernel arm was rejected before
any downstream evaluation, so no larger quantum execution took place.
