\section{What was fixed in advance, and what the gate enforces}

The pre-registration was frozen before any model was fitted and before any block other than
the file header was read. Two of the guarantees claimed in the body are valid only if their
parameters were chosen without reference to the data, so the commitment is mechanical rather
than declarative.

\textbf{Learn-then-Test} controls family-wise error over a \emph{finite grid fixed in
advance}. Enlarging the grid after seeing which value looks good inflates the very error rate
the procedure bounds. \textbf{Split-conformal coverage} separately requires
$\alpha$ to be fixed before the calibration scores are seen; choosing the level after looking
at them moves the order statistic the exact Beta-Binomial law is stated for.

\texttt{scripts/check\_protocol.py} hashes the guarantee-bearing fields of
\texttt{configs/default.yaml} --- every $\alpha$ grid, $\delta$, $\rho$, the decision-grid
size, the FWER method, the band traffic-budget and recall-floor grids, the split fractions and
the decline budget --- and exits non-zero on any undocumented change. It does \emph{not} hash
the protocol's prose: correcting a mis-transcribed count should not require an amendment, and
a gate irritating enough to be bypassed protects nothing. Fields entering no guarantee
(output paths, fitting seeds, plotting choices) are deliberately outside the hash.

\paragraph{Amendments.} The pre-registration carries \ClaimProtocolAmendments{} of them, and
none was derived from $\Dtest$. A1 and A2 come from block sizes and from $\Dband$; A3 and A4
also used quantities measured on $\Dcal$ --- outer-threshold recall, and the band-conditional
rate that fixes its own $\alpha$ scale --- which is disclosed rather than treated as free. The
last five are one correction and four disclosures: a test specified for the wrong mechanism, a
screen computed and never gated, work pre-registered and never done, a rule stated more
strongly than the code enforces it, and a dataset never obtained.

\begin{enumerate}[label=A\arabic*.]
\item The band-conditional certificate is sample-starved by construction; the reachable
  $\alpha$ is capped by the band budget rather than by the method. Recorded before the arm ran,
  and Section~3 of the body reports the measurement landing exactly there.
\item PSD2 SCA-RTS rates withdrawn as a threshold selector. The ceilings are
  \emph{value}-weighted, measured over a rolling 90 days across a whole portfolio, and govern
  exemption eligibility rather than a decline threshold. $\Dband$, the block this was measured on, has a
  value-weighted fraud rate of \ClaimBandValueFraudRate\,\%, \ClaimBandFraudVersusCeiling{} times the loosest
  ceiling of \ClaimPsdLoosestCeiling\,\%; meeting it on this file would
  require declining \ClaimPsdDeclineRate\,\% of traffic. Applying a portfolio ceiling to a fraud-enriched
  research benchmark is a category error, and it was ours.
\item The decline budget moved from 2\,\% to 5\,\%, and the recall floor is reported as a
  frontier rather than a fixed constraint.
\item The band-conditional $\alpha$ scale, the recall-loss reformulation as a false-negative
  rate, and the exclusion of the decision grid's upper endpoint.
\item \emph{Dated after the certificate was produced.} H5's pre-registered test was specified
  for the wrong mechanism --- an exact Beta-Binomial tail is the law for split-conformal
  coverage, not for a Learn-then-Test certificate applied unchanged to a held-out block.
\item \emph{Recorded on discovery.} The geometric-difference screen was pre-registered as an
  a-priori screen and computed but never gated on --- a divergence between protocol and code.
  What rejected the arm was RBF distinctness, which admitted none of the
  \ClaimScreenConfigs{} configurations; conditioning admitted \ClaimScreenPassConditioning{}.
\item \emph{Disclosure of non-execution.} The protocol's drift section lists five measurements
  as ``measured, not assumed''. Three were never run --- the conformal test martingale, the
  block permutation test, and band-conditional against marginal drift. No script exists for any
  of them, and the two tables they would have written do not exist.
\item \emph{The single-evaluation rule described more than the code enforces.} The rule said
  every sweep and ablation runs on held-out slices of $\Dband$ or $\Dcal$; of the six scripts
  that read $\Dtest$, four do so without the guard. Nothing was \emph{selected} there --- the ablation table feeds no
  claim, and every threshold comes from $\Dband$ and $\Dcal$ --- so the guarantee stands, but
  the sentence did not. The protocol was corrected rather than the scripts, which would have
  changed committed tables to retire a wording defect.
\item \emph{Disclosure of a dataset promised and never obtained.} ULB European Cardholder was
  pre-registered as secondary and never downloaded, so the external-validity experiment did not
  run and the counts quoted for it came from its published description rather than from
  measurement. Withdrawn rather than recomputed, which would assert a measurement not made. The
  statement permits one or two of its three datasets; what is disclosed here is the
  pre-registration promising a third, not a scope the challenge required.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The single-evaluation rule is a mechanism, and its scope is selection.} Selection
happens once: band edges and $\lambda$ come from $\Dband$ and $\Dcal$ and are carried to
$\Dtest$ unchanged, and nothing is tuned or ranked there. The loader records the authorised configuration hash in
\texttt{results/tables/test\_access.json} and raises on a second distinct one, so a reviewer
can read the ledger rather than take our word for it. Amendment~A8 lists the scripts that read
the block for reporting without passing through it.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.} H1--H5 were stated in advance with their tests. Only two were tested.
H1, H2 and H3 all compare a quantum kernel against a control, and the a-priori screens rejected
every kernel before any of them could run --- so they are not open questions but questions the
stopping rule closed. H4 --- that the tensor network does not improve band-conditional average
precision over the baseline --- is the one the body reports, and it is reported as
underpowered against its own pre-registered ceiling. H5 is validated on the held-out block.
