\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{What a random split buys you, measured.} Before any conformal machinery, the split
itself. Table~\ref{tab:splitarm} runs one gradient-boosted model at fixed hyperparameters over
five seeds on three splits of the same file. A stratified random split reports
\ClaimSplitApInflation{} more average precision than a forward holdout ---
\ClaimSplitApStratified{} against \ClaimSplitApTemporal{} --- with non-overlapping ranges. That
is the largest effect in this study, larger than anything either quantum arm could have
contributed, and it is why what follows reports a temporal split.

It is also what separates this baseline from the published leaderboard: the 1st-place IEEE-CIS
solution \Cited{reports ROC AUC 0.9459} under time-based cross-validation over the training
file, against \ClaimGbdtFullAucMin--\ClaimGbdtFullAucMax{} here on a strict forward holdout ---
different tasks, and the difference is measured above rather than assumed. The appendix carries
the primary metrics in full.

\paragraph{Coverage fails under time, on every seed, and the controls do not reproduce it.}
Table~\ref{tab:coverageseeds} calibrates on $\Dcal$ and reports on $\Dtest$, five seeds per
arm and level, each judged against the exact Beta-Binomial interval. The temporal arm falls
outside on \emph{every} seed at the two loosest levels and on \ClaimTemporalOutsideMid{} of
\ClaimCoverageSeeds{} at the third, and inside on every seed at the tightest; the
stratified arm falls outside on none, at any level.

The card-disjoint arm is not clean, and the two levels fail differently. At $\alpha = 0.01$ it
is outside on \ClaimCardDisjointOutsideLoosest{} of \ClaimCoverageSeeds{} seeds and both
\emph{over}-cover --- a two-sided fit failure leaving the one-sided guarantee intact. At
$\alpha = 0.001$ it is outside on \ClaimCardDisjointOutsideTightest{} of
\ClaimCoverageSeeds{}, and that seed runs the other way: ratio
\ClaimCardDisjointTightestWorstRatio{} against four below one, the lowest
\ClaimCardDisjointTightestBestRatio{}. One against four reads as noise, but it is
under-coverage, so this arm is not reported as a pass; its spread
(sd \ClaimCardDisjointSdTightest) is an order of magnitude wider than the stratified arm's.
What the controls establish is narrower than an earlier draft claimed and still sufficient:
neither reproduces the temporal arm's every-seed breach. That is weaker than ``the breach is
temporal'' --- the arms differ in more than time --- and it is what three arms support.

It is not label censoring: the trailing-window fraud rate is
\ClaimCensoringTrailingRate\,\% against \ClaimCensoringEarlierRate\,\% earlier --- higher, not
lower --- with no monotone trend ($\tau = \ClaimCensoringTau$, $p = \ClaimCensoringP$).

\paragraph{But the breach is origin-dependent, and this narrows the claim.} Re-running at
five rolling calibration origins gives Table~\ref{tab:rolling}: the ratio ranges from
\ClaimRollingRatioMin{} to \ClaimRollingRatioMax. \ClaimRollingBreaches{} of
\ClaimRollingOrigins{} breach, and the earliest is \emph{conservative} --- the deviation
reverses direction. So the defensible statement is not that temporal splits inflate the
false-decline rate by a fixed factor, but that the deviation depends on where the calibration
window falls, and a single origin cannot establish its size.

\paragraph{And it holds on the held-out block.} This is H5. All \ClaimHFiveConfigsTotal{}
certified configurations were applied to $\Dtest$ unchanged --- same edges, same $\lambda$, no
recalibration --- and \ClaimHFiveConfigsHolding{} of \ClaimHFiveConfigsTotal{} realised a
band-conditional false-decline rate at or below their certified $\alpha$, the tightest using
\ClaimHFiveWorstRatio{} of its budget. Holding a ceiling that loose is a real check that the
machinery transfers and a weak one, which amendment~A1 predicted from the band's sample size
before the arm ran.

\paragraph{What the certificate actually reaches.} \ClaimCertifiedConfigs{} of \ClaimRiskConfigs{} configurations certify, all
strictly inside the band --- the smallest margin below $\tauhi$ is \ClaimCertificateMargin{}.
That is not automatic: a $\lambda$ at the band edge flags nothing, so its risk is structurally
zero and the guarantee vacuous. An earlier version of this table was exactly that.

The survivors need $\alpha \ge 0.10$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{FN}} = 0.45$, and nothing certifies
at the tightest band budget --- where $\Dcal$ contributes only \ClaimBandLegitCalMin{}
legitimate in-band rows against \ClaimCalLegitCount{} overall. The binding mechanism is sample
size acting through the concentration bound, not the class-conditional degeneracy floor.
