\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{What a random split buys you, measured.} Before any conformal machinery, the
split itself. Table~\ref{tab:splitarm} runs one gradient-boosted model at fixed
hyperparameters over five seeds on three splits of the same file. A stratified random split
reports \ClaimSplitApInflation{} more average precision than a forward holdout ---
\ClaimSplitApStratified{} against \ClaimSplitApTemporal{} --- with ranges that do not
overlap across seeds.

That is the single largest effect in this study, it is larger than anything either quantum
arm could have contributed, and it requires no quantum method to observe. It is also why the
rest of this section reports a temporal split even though a random one would flatter every
number in it.

\paragraph{Coverage fails under time, on every seed, and the controls do not reproduce it.}
Table~\ref{tab:coverageseeds} calibrates on $\Dcal$ and reports on $\Dtest$, five seeds per
arm and level, each judged against the exact Beta-Binomial interval. The temporal arm falls
outside on \emph{every} seed at the two loosest levels and on four of five at the third; the
stratified arm falls outside on none, at any level.

The card-disjoint arm is not clean, and saying so matters: it is outside on two of five seeds
at $\alpha = 0.01$ and on one of five at $\alpha = 0.001$, where its seed-to-seed spread
(sd \ClaimCardDisjointSdTightest) is an order of magnitude wider than the temporal arm's. An earlier draft of this
paragraph said both controls sat inside at every level, which the five-seed data does not
support. What the controls establish is narrower and still sufficient: neither reproduces the
temporal arm's systematic, every-seed breach at the levels where it occurs.

The three-arm design exists to decompose degradation into a temporal component and a sampling
component. The decomposition it supports is this: the breach appears in the arm that orders
blocks in time, on every seed, and neither control reproduces it. That is weaker than "the
breach is temporal" --- the arms differ in more than time, and the card-disjoint arm is in
fact the most distinguishable of the three by a two-sample classifier --- but it is what three
non-randomised conditions can carry.

\paragraph{But the breach is origin-dependent, and this narrows the claim.} Re-running at
five rolling calibration origins gives Table~\ref{tab:rolling}: the ratio ranges from
\ClaimRollingRatioMin{} to \ClaimRollingRatioMax. Two of five breach, and the earliest is
\emph{conservative} --- the deviation reverses direction. So the defensible statement is not
that temporal splits inflate the false-decline rate by a fixed factor; it is that the
deviation depends on where the calibration window falls, and a single origin cannot establish
its size. An earlier draft of this work quoted a single seed's maximum as a central estimate.
That was an overclaim, and correcting it removed our cleanest headline number.

\paragraph{And it holds on the held-out block.} All \ClaimHFiveConfigsTotal{} certified
configurations were applied unchanged to $\Dtest$ --- same band edges, same $\lambda$, no
recalibration --- and \ClaimHFiveConfigsHolding{} of \ClaimHFiveConfigsTotal{} realised a
band-conditional false-decline rate at or below the $\alpha$ they were certified at. The
tightest used \ClaimHFiveWorstRatio{} of its budget.

That is H5, and until this round it had never been run: the certificate was the study's
headline deliverable and had no held-out evidence behind it at all. The result should be read
for what it is. Holding a 0.10-to-0.25 risk ceiling is a real check that the machinery
transfers, and it is a weak one, because those ceilings are loose --- which is what
amendment~A1 predicted from the band's sample size before the arm ran.

\paragraph{What the certificate actually reaches.} \ClaimCertifiedConfigs{} of \ClaimRiskConfigs{} configurations certify, all
strictly inside the band --- the smallest margin below $\tauhi$ is \ClaimCertificateMargin. That property is
not automatic: an earlier version certified a larger set, every one of them
sitting exactly at the band edge, where the flagged set is empty by construction and the risk
is structurally zero. The certificate was vacuous and we did not notice until we asked where
the selected $\lambda$ sat.

The surviving configurations need $\alpha \ge 0.10$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{FN}} = 0.45$.
Nothing certifies at the tightest band budget at all. The mechanism is sample size, not
method: at a 2\,\% traffic budget $\Dcal$ contributes only \ClaimBandLegitCalMin{} legitimate in-band rows
against \ClaimCalLegitCount{} overall, and the class-conditional degeneracy floor
$\lceil 1/\alpha\rceil - 1$ then binds. We pre-registered this limitation as an amendment
before running the arm, and the measurement landed where the amendment predicted.
