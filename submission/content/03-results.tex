\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{What a random split buys you, measured.} Table~\ref{tab:splitarm} runs one
gradient-boosted model at fixed hyperparameters over five seeds on three splits of the same
file. A stratified random split reports \ClaimSplitApInflation{} more average precision than a
forward holdout --- \ClaimSplitApStratified{} against \ClaimSplitApTemporal{}, non-overlapping
across seeds. That is the largest effect in this study, larger than anything either quantum arm
could have contributed, and it is why what follows reports a temporal split.

It is also what separates this baseline from the published leaderboard. The 1st-place IEEE-CIS
solution \Cited{reports ROC AUC 0.9459} on the private leaderboard --- the competition's own
held-out file --- and about \Cited{0.9363} in time-based cross-validation over the training
file, against \ClaimGbdtFullAucMin--\ClaimGbdtFullAucMax{} here on a strict forward holdout of
that training file. Different tasks, measured above rather than assumed; the appendix carries
the primary metrics in full.

\paragraph{Coverage fails under time, on every seed, and the controls do not reproduce it.}
Table~\ref{tab:coverageseeds} calibrates on $\Dcal$ and reports on $\Dtest$, five seeds per
arm and level, each judged against the exact Beta-Binomial interval. The temporal arm falls
outside on \emph{every} seed at the two loosest levels and on \ClaimTemporalOutsideMid{} of
\ClaimCoverageSeeds{} at the third, and inside on every seed at the tightest; the
stratified arm falls outside on none, at any level.

The card-disjoint arm is not clean either way: it \emph{over}-covers on
\ClaimCardDisjointOutsideLoosest{} of \ClaimCoverageSeeds{} seeds at $\alpha = 0.01$ and
\emph{under}-covers on \ClaimCardDisjointOutsideTightest{} at $\alpha = 0.001$, with a spread
(sd \ClaimCardDisjointSdTightest) an order of magnitude wider than the temporal arm's, so it is
not reported as a pass. Neither control reproduces the temporal arm's every-seed breach, which
is weaker than ``the breach is temporal'' because the arms differ in more than time. Label
censoring is ruled out separately: the trailing-window rate is
\ClaimCensoringTrailingRate\,\% against \ClaimCensoringEarlierRate\,\% earlier.

\paragraph{But the breach is origin-dependent, and this narrows the claim.} Re-running at
five rolling calibration origins gives Table~\ref{tab:rolling}: the ratio ranges from
\ClaimRollingRatioMin{} to \ClaimRollingRatioMax. \ClaimRollingBreaches{} of
\ClaimRollingOrigins{} breach, and the earliest is \emph{conservative} --- the deviation
reverses direction. So the defensible statement is not a fixed inflation factor but that the
deviation depends on where the calibration window falls, which one origin cannot establish.

\paragraph{And it holds on the held-out block.} This is H5. All \ClaimHFiveConfigsTotal{}
certified configurations were applied to $\Dtest$ unchanged --- same edges, same $\lambda$ ---
and \ClaimHFiveConfigsHolding{} of \ClaimHFiveConfigsTotal{} realised a band-conditional
false-decline rate at or below their certified $\alpha$, the tightest using
\ClaimHFiveWorstRatio{} of its budget. Holding a ceiling that loose is a real check that the
machinery transfers and a weak one, which amendment~A1 predicted before the arm ran.

\paragraph{What the certificate actually reaches.} \ClaimCertifiedConfigs{} of \ClaimRiskConfigs{} configurations certify, all
strictly inside the band --- the smallest margin below $\tauhi$ is \ClaimCertificateMargin{}.
That is not automatic: a $\lambda$ at the band edge flags nothing, so its risk is structurally
zero and the guarantee vacuous --- as an earlier version of this table was.

The survivors need $\alpha \ge 0.10$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{FN}} = 0.45$, and nothing certifies
at the tightest band budget --- where $\Dcal$ contributes only \ClaimBandLegitCalMin{}
legitimate in-band rows against \ClaimCalLegitCount{} overall. The binding mechanism is sample
size acting through the concentration bound, not the class-conditional degeneracy floor.
